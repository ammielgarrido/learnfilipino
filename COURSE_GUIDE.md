# Learn Filipino Course Guide

This file is the main curriculum reference for the Learn Filipino website. Update it whenever a lesson, checkpoint, difficulty rule, or progress feature changes.

## Learning approach

- Teach 10 useful words at a time.
- Show an English meaning, a Filipino word, a child-friendly visual, and a short spoken example.
- Let the learner mark words as practiced.
- End every lesson with a 10-question quiz.
- Require 8/10 to complete a lesson.
- After every two lessons, test every word learned so far in a cumulative checkpoint.
- Require 80% to complete a checkpoint.
- Increase difficulty from single words to phrases, listening, recall, and simple conversation.

## Progress saving

The current website uses browser `localStorage` and saves:

- practiced words for each lesson;
- best lesson-quiz score;
- lesson completion;
- best checkpoint score;
- checkpoint completion; and
- the most recently opened lesson.

This works without an account but stays on the same browser and device. Clearing browser data removes it. The future cross-device version should add a parent account and cloud database while keeping local saving as an offline fallback.

## Difficulty roadmap

### Level 3: Everyday vocabulary

Suggested lessons: family members, food and drinks, body parts, clothes, animals, colors, numbers, days, and weather.

Add reverse questions: show the Filipino word and ask for the English meaning. Mix forward and reverse questions in checkpoints.

### Level 4: Useful phrases

Combine known words into short patterns such as `Ito ang...`, `Gusto ko...`, `Nasaan ang...`, and `Pupunta ako sa...`. Add sentence-building and missing-word questions.

### Level 5: Listening and speaking

Play audio without showing the word, then ask the learner to choose the matching picture. Later, add optional microphone practice with clear parent permission and child-privacy safeguards.

### Level 6: Mini conversations

Use short scenarios such as greeting family, asking for food, describing feelings, finding an object, or visiting a place. Mix vocabulary, listening, and sentence comprehension.

## Content checklist for every new lesson

1. Choose one child-relevant theme and exactly 10 words.
2. Verify spelling, meaning, natural usage, and regional alternatives with a fluent Filipino speaker.
3. Add a clear visual and one short example phrase per word.
4. Check pronunciation audio on mobile and desktop.
5. Add the lesson to the correct cumulative checkpoint.
6. Confirm the 80% passing rule and unlocking sequence.
7. Test progress after refreshing and reopening the browser.
8. Update this guide before publishing.
