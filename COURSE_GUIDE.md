# Learn Filipino Course Guide

This file is the main curriculum reference for the Learn Filipino website. Update it whenever a lesson, checkpoint, difficulty rule, or progress feature changes.

## Learning approach

- Teach 10 useful words or phrases at a time.
- Show an English meaning, a Filipino word, a child-friendly visual, and a short spoken example.
- Let the learner mark words as practiced.
- End every lesson with a 10-question quiz.
- Require 8/10 to complete a lesson.
- After every two lessons, give a checkpoint. Levels 1–4 are cumulative; Level 5 tests the two new listening lessons.
- Require 80% to complete a checkpoint.
- Increase difficulty from single words to phrases, listening, recall, and simple conversation.
- Spiral older material into new lessons instead of teaching a topic only once.
- Develop listening, speaking, reading, writing, viewing, and cultural understanding together.
- State a child-friendly “I can” outcome for every level and assess the learner by using Filipino in context, not only by translating words.

## Curriculum framework

This is a custom home-learning curriculum, not an official school credential. Its structure is informed by three complementary frameworks:

- The Philippine Department of Education MATATAG curriculum, which prioritizes foundational literacy and communicative ability and organizes Filipino around literacy, language, and text.
- DepEd early-language guidance, which develops oral language, phonological awareness, print knowledge, phonics, fluency, composition, grammar, vocabulary, listening comprehension, and reading comprehension.
- International proficiency frameworks from ACTFL and the CEFR, which describe progress through real-world “can do” abilities across listening, speaking, reading, writing, interaction, and mediation.

Every level should therefore include five strands:

1. **Meaning and vocabulary:** useful words grouped around a child-relevant situation.
2. **Listening and pronunciation:** human audio, sound discrimination, and comprehension without printed clues.
3. **Speaking and interaction:** repeating, answering, asking, choosing, and eventually creating original responses.
4. **Reading and writing:** sounds and syllables first, then words, sentences, stories, and short original texts.
5. **Language and culture:** grammar in meaningful sentences, respectful language, Filipino family and community life, songs, stories, and regional variation.

Filipino verbs should be taught primarily as **aspect**, not as a direct copy of English tense. The course will explicitly teach completed (`kumain`), ongoing (`kumakain`), and contemplated/future (`kakain`) actions, together with time words such as `kahapon`, `ngayon`, `mamaya`, and `bukas`.

Phrase quizzes hide the final noun for missing-word questions. Sentence-building questions let learners tap shuffled words, undo the last selection, and submit when all words are selected. Each lesson includes five of each type. The Level 4 checkpoint includes all 80 entries once, with ten questions of each phrase type; passing requires 64/80.

### Lesson 9: Listen for everyday words

Reviews ten familiar words by sound: `Masaya`, `Takot`, `Bahay`, `Kutsara`, `Paaralan`, `Palengke`, `Guro`, `Magsasaka`, `Nanay`, and `Tubig`. The quiz hides the Filipino text and asks the learner to choose among four picture-and-English options.

### Lesson 10: Listen for useful sentences

Reviews ten familiar sentences by sound, using the Level 4 patterns `Ito ang…`, `Gusto ko ng…`, `Nasaan ang…?`, and `Pupunta ako sa…`. The quiz hides the Filipino sentence and asks the learner to choose the matching picture and English meaning.

The Level 5 checkpoint combines all 20 listening prompts. Every question includes a replay button. The browser uses a Filipino device voice when one is available, so pronunciation must be checked on the learner's actual phone, tablet, or computer.

## Progress saving

The current website uses browser `localStorage` and saves:

- practiced words for each lesson;
- best lesson-quiz score;
- lesson completion;
- best checkpoint score;
- checkpoint completion; and
- the most recently opened lesson.

This works without an account but stays on the same browser and device. Clearing browser data removes it. The future cross-device version should add a parent account and cloud database while keeping local saving as an offline fallback.

## Long-term school-like progression

The sequence below is the curriculum backbone. Topics may be divided into additional small lessons when a child needs more practice; mastery is more important than speed.

| Phase | Levels | Main learning | Example learner outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foundations | 1–5 | Familiar vocabulary, useful sentence patterns, listening discrimination | “I can recognize and understand familiar words and short sentences.” |
| Social communication | 6–8 | Greetings, introductions, family talk, needs, courtesy (`po`, `opo`), simple questions and answers | “I can take part in a short practiced conversation.” |
| Sentence foundations | 9–11 | Pronouns; `ang`, `ng`, `sa`, `si`, `mga`; possession; demonstratives; location; description; negation; question words | “I can make and understand simple original sentences.” |
| Reading foundations | 12–14 | Filipino sounds, syllables, spelling, high-frequency words, sentence reading, handwriting/typing | “I can decode and write familiar Filipino words and sentences.” |
| Actions and time | 15–18 | Verb roots; completed, ongoing, and contemplated aspects; time signals; daily routines; commands and requests | “I can say what happened, what is happening, and what will happen.” |
| Expanding grammar | 19–22 | Actor-focus patterns (`-um-`, `mag-`), introductory object-focus patterns (`-in-`, `i-`), linkers `na/-ng`, connectors, comparison, quantity | “I can combine ideas and choose common verb patterns in familiar situations.” |
| Connected texts | 23–26 | Sequencing events, short stories, informational text, retelling, main idea, details, prediction, cause and effect | “I can understand and retell a short age-appropriate text.” |
| Independent beginner | 27–30 | Unrehearsed conversation, short messages and descriptions, practical reading, culture projects, mixed-skill review | “I can communicate about everyday life with connected sentences.” |

### Planned content by level

- **Level 6 — Mini conversations:** greetings, introductions, asking for food, describing feelings, and finding an object.
- **Level 7 — People and politeness:** personal pronouns (`ako`, `ikaw`, `siya`, `kami`, `tayo`, `kayo`, `sila`), names, `po/opo`, and turn-taking.
- **Level 8 — Questions and needs:** `ano`, `sino`, `saan`, `ilan`, `kailan`, `bakit`, and `paano`; yes/no answers; `gusto`, `ayaw`, and polite requests.
- **Level 9 — Things and ownership:** `ang`, `ng`, `sa`, `si`, `mga`; `ko`, `mo`, `niya`, `namin`, `natin`, `ninyo`, and `nila` in age-appropriate patterns.
- **Level 10 — Description:** colors, size, shape, body, clothes, animals, adjective linkers `na/-ng`, and simple comparisons.
- **Level 11 — Place and existence:** `ito/iyan/iyon`, `dito/diyan/doon`, `may/mayroon/wala`, location phrases, and `hindi`/`wala` negation.
- **Level 12 — Sounds and syllables:** Filipino alphabet and sound-symbol relationships, syllable blending, stress awareness, and spelling familiar words.
- **Level 13 — Reading sentences:** high-frequency function words, punctuation, short commands, questions, and decodable sentences.
- **Level 14 — Writing foundations:** copying, dictation, labeling pictures, completing sentences, and writing one original sentence.
- **Level 15 — Action roots and routines:** common roots such as `kain`, `inom`, `tulog`, `laro`, `basa`, `sulat`, `lakad`, and `takbo` used in daily routines.
- **Level 16 — Ongoing aspect:** actions happening now, such as `kumakain`, `umiinom`, `naglalaro`, and `nagbabasa`, with `ngayon`.
- **Level 17 — Completed aspect:** past/completed actions such as `kumain`, `uminom`, `naglaro`, and `nagbasa`, with `kahapon` and `kanina`.
- **Level 18 — Contemplated aspect:** intended/future actions such as `kakain`, `iinom`, `maglalaro`, and `magbabasa`, with `mamaya` and `bukas`; finish with a three-aspect comparison checkpoint.
- **Levels 19–22 — Expanding grammar:** introduce common focus patterns through meaning and repeated examples before using formal grammar labels; add commands, requests, connectors (`at`, `o`, `pero`, `dahil`, `kaya`), quantity, and comparison.
- **Levels 23–26 — Reading and stories:** short illustrated stories and informational passages; identify characters, setting, sequence, main idea, details, cause/effect, and retell orally and in writing.
- **Levels 27–30 — Independent use:** role-play everyday situations, understand short authentic audio, read practical texts, write short connected messages, and complete family/culture projects.

### Assessment progression

- Keep 10-item lesson quizzes and 80% mastery for focused practice.
- After every two lessons, include a checkpoint that spirals important older material.
- After every four levels, add a performance task: a recorded conversation, oral retelling, reading sample, or short written product.
- Measure listening, speaking, reading, and writing separately so strong recognition does not hide a production gap.
- Use “I can” statements and a simple parent rubric: **not yet**, **with help**, **independently**, and **independently in a new situation**.
- Allow review without penalty and schedule retrieval after one day, one week, and one month.
- Do not unlock a major new grammar phase based only on multiple-choice scores; require at least one comprehension task and one production task.

## Content checklist for every new lesson

1. Choose one child-relevant theme and usually 10 words, phrases, sound patterns, or short text targets.
2. Verify spelling, meaning, natural usage, and regional alternatives with a fluent Filipino speaker.
3. Add a clear visual and one short example phrase per word.
4. Check pronunciation audio on mobile and desktop.
5. Add the lesson to the correct cumulative checkpoint.
6. Confirm the 80% passing rule and unlocking sequence.
7. Test progress after refreshing and reopening the browser.
8. Update this guide before publishing.
9. State the listening, speaking, reading, writing, and cultural outcomes covered by the lesson.
10. Add an “I can” outcome and ensure the assessment actually measures it.
